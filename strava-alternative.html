Why athletes look for a Strava alternative

Strava is great for kudos and segments with friends. But your training history is years of effort, and many athletes want it somewhere they control.

  • Your data, your server. Every activity lives in a database on your own hardware or VPS. No third party decides what you can see, export or keep.
  • No paywall on your own stats. Heatmaps, best efforts, gear tracking, yearly reviews: all included. There is no premium tier, now or later.
  • Not locked to one platform. Import from FIT, TCX and GPX files or sync from Garmin, Polar, Wahoo, Hammerhead and Zepp. Strava becomes optional.
  • Private by design. No user accounts on our side, no ads, no tracking. Your rides and runs are only visible to you, unless you choose otherwise.
Step by step

How to move your Strava history to Dreeve

Strava lets every user download a full archive of their account, including the original activity files. Dreeve imports those files directly. You don't need a Strava subscription for this.

  1. 1

    Request your Strava archive

    On strava.com, go to Settings → My Account → Download or Delete Your Account and choose Request your archive. Strava emails you a download link, usually within a few hours.

  2. 2

    Install Dreeve

    If you can run a Docker container, you can run Dreeve: a home server, a NAS or a small VPS all work.

    bash
    $ mkdir dreeve && cd dreeve # add docker-compose.yml and .env from the installation guide $ docker compose up -d # then open http://localhost:8080

    Full instructions are in the installation guide.

  3. 3

    Unpack your activity files and drop them into the watch folder

    Dreeve reads plain .fit, .tcx and .gpx files, not compressed archives. Unzip the Strava archive, then decompress the activity files inside it: Strava stores many of them as .fit.gz, .gpx.gz or .tcx.gz. Finally, copy them into Dreeve's watch/ folder.

    bash
    $ unzip export_*.zip -d strava-export $ gunzip strava-export/activities/*.gz $ cp strava-export/activities/*.{fit,tcx,gpx} /path/to/dreeve/watch/

    On Windows or macOS you can use 7-Zip or The Unarchiver instead. FIT files give you the richest data: power, heart rate, laps and full streams.

  4. 4

    Let Dreeve import everything

    Dreeve checks the folder every five minutes, or you can start an import right away with docker compose exec app bin/console app:import:files. Duplicates are skipped automatically, so it's safe to re-run. You can follow progress on the import log at /admin/file-imports.

  5. 5

    Keep new activities flowing in

    Connect Garmin Connect, Polar Flow, Wahoo or another connector so new workouts arrive straight from your device. No Strava in between.

Frequently asked questions

Is Dreeve a free Strava alternative?

Yes. Dreeve is free and open source under the AGPL-3.0 licence. There is no premium tier and no paywall on any statistic. You run it yourself with Docker.

Do I need a Strava account to use Dreeve?

No. Dreeve works with plain FIT, TCX and GPX files and with connectors for Garmin Connect, Polar Flow, Wahoo, Hammerhead and Zepp. Strava sync is optional.

How do I move my Strava history to Dreeve?

Request your account archive from Strava, unzip it, decompress the .gz activity files inside, and drop the FIT, TCX and GPX files into Dreeve's watch folder. Dreeve imports them automatically and skips duplicates, so you can re-run the import safely. See the step-by-step guide above.

Can I keep using Strava alongside Dreeve?

Yes. Many people keep Strava for its social features and use Dreeve as a private, long-term archive and analysis tool. Dreeve can sync from Strava directly if you have a Strava subscription.

Where is my data stored?

On your own server, in a local database. Dreeve has no user accounts on our side, no tracking and no ads.

What do I need to run Dreeve?

Anything that can run a Docker container: a home server, a NAS, a Raspberry Pi-class machine or a small VPS. Kubernetes is supported too.
